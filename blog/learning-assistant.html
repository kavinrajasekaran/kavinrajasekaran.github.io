From Student to Learning Assistant: Teaching Electrical Engineering and Physics

January 18, 2025

Transitioning from being a student to becoming a learning assistant has been one of the most rewarding experiences during my time at UC Merced. Working as a TA for both electrical engineering and physics courses gave me a unique perspective on how these fundamental sciences interconnect and how to help students build intuition for complex concepts.

My Journey into Teaching

I never initially planned to become a teaching assistant. Like many students, I was focused on getting through my coursework and building technical skills. However, after excelling in several physics and electrical engineering courses, my professors encouraged me to apply for TA positions.

What started as a way to gain some teaching experience quickly became a passion. There's something incredibly satisfying about seeing a student's face light up when they finally understand a concept they've been struggling with.

Teaching Electrical Engineering: Making the Abstract Concrete

Electrical engineering can be particularly challenging for students because many of the concepts are abstract. You can't see current flowing through a wire or visualize electromagnetic fields the way you can observe mechanical motion.

Circuit Analysis: Building Intuition

When teaching circuit analysis, I found that students often got lost in the mathematics without developing physical intuition. My approach focused on:

  • Water analogies: Comparing voltage to pressure, current to flow rate, and resistance to pipe diameter
  • Energy perspectives: Always asking "where is the energy coming from and where is it going?"
  • Limiting cases: What happens when resistance approaches zero or infinity?
  • Physical validation: Do the numbers make sense in terms of power consumption and heat dissipation?

Signals and Systems: Connecting Math to Reality

Fourier transforms and convolution can seem like pure mathematics, but they have profound physical meaning. I helped students understand:

  • Frequency domain thinking: Every signal is made up of sinusoids at different frequencies
  • System behavior: How filters affect different frequency components
  • Real-world applications: From audio processing to communication systems

Teaching Physics: The Foundation of Understanding

Physics provides the fundamental principles that underlie all engineering disciplines. As a physics TA, I focused on helping students develop problem-solving strategies and physical intuition.

Classical Mechanics: Newton's Laws in Action

Many students memorize formulas without understanding when and why to apply them. My teaching approach emphasized:

  • Free body diagrams: The foundation of all mechanics problems
  • Energy conservation: Often the most elegant path to a solution
  • Symmetry arguments: Using physical reasoning to simplify complex problems
  • Dimensional analysis: Checking that equations make physical sense

Electromagnetism: Bridging Physics and Engineering

Electromagnetism is where physics and electrical engineering meet most directly. Key concepts I focused on:

  • Field visualization: Using field lines and equipotential surfaces to build intuition
  • Maxwell's equations: The four fundamental laws that govern all electromagnetic phenomena
  • Wave propagation: How electromagnetic waves carry energy and information
  • Engineering applications: From transformers to antennas to optical fibers

The Interconnection: Where Physics Meets Engineering

One of the most valuable aspects of working in both departments was helping students see the connections between physics and engineering:

Quantum Mechanics → Semiconductor Devices

Understanding band theory and quantum tunneling is essential for modern electronics. I helped students connect:

  • Wave-particle duality to electron behavior in crystals
  • Energy bands to semiconductor properties
  • Quantum tunneling to tunnel diodes and flash memory

Thermodynamics → Power Systems

Energy conversion and heat transfer principles directly apply to:

  • Power plant efficiency calculations
  • Thermal management in electronic systems
  • Battery chemistry and performance

Teaching Strategies That Work

Through my experience as a learning assistant, I developed several effective teaching strategies:

1. Start with Physical Intuition

Before diving into equations, help students understand what's physically happening. Use analogies, visualizations, and thought experiments to build intuition.

2. Encourage Active Problem-Solving

Instead of showing students how to solve problems, guide them to discover solutions themselves. Ask leading questions and let them work through the reasoning.

3. Connect to Real-World Applications

Students are more motivated when they see how concepts apply to real engineering problems. Use examples from current technology and research.

4. Emphasize Multiple Approaches

Show students that there are often multiple ways to solve a problem. This builds flexibility and deeper understanding.

Challenges and Rewards

Common Student Struggles

  • Mathematical anxiety: Fear of complex equations overshadowing physical understanding
  • Memorization vs. understanding: Focusing on formulas rather than concepts
  • Abstraction difficulties: Trouble visualizing invisible phenomena like fields and waves
  • Problem-solving strategies: Not knowing where to start with complex problems

Breakthrough Moments

The most rewarding moments came when students had genuine "aha!" experiences:

  • Realizing that all of electronics follows from basic physics principles
  • Understanding how mathematical tools like Fourier transforms represent physical reality
  • Connecting classroom concepts to technology they use every day
  • Developing confidence to tackle new, unfamiliar problems

What I Learned About Learning

Teaching taught me as much about learning as it did about my subject areas:

  • Everyone learns differently: Visual, auditory, and kinesthetic learners need different approaches
  • Patience is crucial: Concepts that seem obvious to you may be completely foreign to students
  • Questions reveal understanding: The questions students ask show you exactly where their mental models break down
  • Teaching deepens knowledge: Explaining concepts to others forces you to understand them at a deeper level

Impact Beyond the Classroom

The skills I developed as a learning assistant have been invaluable in my other work:

  • Communication: Explaining complex technical concepts clearly and concisely
  • Patience and empathy: Understanding different perspectives and learning styles
  • Problem decomposition: Breaking complex problems into manageable pieces
  • Adaptive thinking: Approaching problems from multiple angles when one approach doesn't work

Advice for Future Learning Assistants

If you're considering becoming a TA or learning assistant, here's my advice:

  • Remember being a student: Don't forget what it was like to struggle with these concepts
  • Prepare thoroughly: Review material before each session, anticipate common questions
  • Listen more than you speak: Understanding student confusion is more important than showing off your knowledge
  • Embrace the challenge: Teaching will push you to understand concepts more deeply than ever before

Looking Forward

My experience as a learning assistant has convinced me that education and mentorship will always be important parts of my career. Whether I'm explaining cryptographic algorithms to fellow researchers or helping junior engineers understand system design, the skills I developed teaching physics and electrical engineering continue to serve me well.

There's something uniquely fulfilling about helping others learn and grow. In a field that's constantly evolving, the ability to teach and learn from others isn't just valuable—it's essential.
