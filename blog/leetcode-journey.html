LeetCode Journey: From Struggling with Arrays to Landing Interviews

November 5, 2024

Like many computer science students, my relationship with LeetCode started out rocky. I remember staring at "Two Sum" for what felt like hours, convinced that there had to be some trick I was missing. Fast forward two years, and algorithmic problem-solving has become one of my favorite aspects of programming.

The Intimidating Beginning

When I first opened LeetCode as a sophomore, I felt completely overwhelmed. The problems seemed to require some mystical algorithmic knowledge that I didn't possess. Even the "Easy" problems felt impossibly difficult.

My first attempt at "Two Sum" involved nested loops and took me 45 minutes to get right. When I saw the optimal O(n) solution using a hash map, I felt like I was looking at magic.

Early Mistakes

  • Jumping to hard problems: I thought I needed to solve difficult problems to improve quickly
  • Not understanding fundamentals: I was trying to memorize solutions instead of understanding patterns
  • Giving up too quickly: I'd look at the solution after 10-15 minutes of being stuck
  • Not tracking progress: I wasn't systematic about what I was learning

Finding My Rhythm

Everything changed when I shifted my approach from "solving as many problems as possible" to "understanding fundamental patterns." Here's what worked for me:

1. Start with Data Structure Fundamentals

Before diving into algorithms, I made sure I truly understood basic data structures:

  • Arrays and Strings: Two pointers, sliding window, prefix sums
  • Hash Tables: When to use them for O(1) lookups
  • Linked Lists: Manipulation techniques, cycle detection
  • Stacks and Queues: When LIFO/FIFO properties are useful

2. Pattern Recognition Over Memorization

Instead of memorizing specific solutions, I focused on recognizing patterns:

  • Two Pointers: Useful for sorted arrays, finding pairs
  • Sliding Window: Subarray/substring problems with constraints
  • Binary Search: Not just for sorted arrays, but for optimization problems
  • DFS/BFS: Tree and graph traversals, pathfinding

3. The 15-Minute Rule

I developed a systematic approach to each problem:

  1. 5 minutes: Understand the problem, identify constraints, think of brute force
  2. 10 minutes: Try to optimize, identify patterns, implement solution
  3. If stuck: Look at hints, not the full solution
  4. After solving: Study the optimal solution and understand why it's better

Key Breakthrough Moments

Understanding Binary Search

Binary search was a major breakthrough for me. Once I understood it wasn't just about finding elements in sorted arrays, but about searching in a solution space, it opened up a whole new category of problems.

Problems like "Find Minimum in Rotated Sorted Array" and "Search in Rotated Sorted Array" suddenly made sense. The key insight was that you're always eliminating half the search space based on some condition.

Mastering Two Pointers

The two-pointer technique became my Swiss Army knife. It's elegant, efficient, and applicable to so many problems:

  • Array problems: Finding pairs, triplets, removing duplicates
  • String problems: Palindrome checking, substring matching
  • Linked list problems: Cycle detection, finding middle element

Dynamic Programming Breakthrough

DP was the hardest concept for me to grasp. The breakthrough came when I stopped trying to memorize DP patterns and started focusing on the problem-solving process:

  1. Identify overlapping subproblems: Are we solving the same smaller problems repeatedly?
  2. Define the recurrence relation: How does the answer to a problem relate to smaller subproblems?
  3. Determine base cases: What are the simplest problems we can solve directly?
  4. Choose implementation: Top-down (memoization) or bottom-up (tabulation)

My LeetCode Strategy

Daily Practice Routine

  • Weekdays: 1-2 problems, focusing on one pattern at a time
  • Weekends: 3-4 problems, mix of review and new concepts
  • Mock interviews: Timed problem-solving sessions once a week

Problem Selection Strategy

  • 70% problems I can solve in 15-20 minutes: Build confidence and speed
  • 20% challenging problems: Push my boundaries
  • 10% review of previously solved problems: Ensure retention

Tracking Progress

I maintained a simple spreadsheet tracking:

  • Problem name and difficulty
  • Pattern/topic (two pointers, DP, etc.)
  • Time taken to solve
  • Whether I needed hints
  • Key insights or mistakes

Common Patterns and When to Use Them

Two Pointers

When to use: Sorted arrays, finding pairs/triplets, palindromes

Key insight: Move pointers based on some condition to avoid nested loops

Example problems: Two Sum II, 3Sum, Container With Most Water

Sliding Window

When to use: Subarray/substring problems with size or condition constraints

Key insight: Expand and contract window to maintain validity

Example problems: Longest Substring Without Repeating Characters, Minimum Window Substring

Binary Search

When to use: Sorted data, optimization problems, finding boundaries

Key insight: Eliminate half the search space each iteration

Example problems: Search Insert Position, Find Peak Element, Median of Two Sorted Arrays

Depth-First Search (DFS)

When to use: Tree/graph traversal, backtracking, path finding

Key insight: Explore as far as possible before backtracking

Example problems: Binary Tree Inorder Traversal, Number of Islands, Word Search

Dynamic Programming

When to use: Optimization problems with overlapping subproblems

Key insight: Build solution from smaller subproblems

Example problems: Climbing Stairs, Coin Change, Longest Increasing Subsequence

Interview Preparation Strategy

Mock Interviews

Regular mock interviews were crucial for interview preparation:

  • Pramp: Free peer-to-peer mock interviews
  • InterviewBit: Structured mock interview platform
  • Friends/classmates: Practice explaining solutions out loud

Communication Practice

Technical interviews aren't just about solving problems—communication is key:

  • Think out loud: Explain your thought process as you work
  • Ask clarifying questions: Confirm assumptions about input constraints
  • Start with brute force: Show you can solve the problem, then optimize
  • Test your solution: Walk through examples to catch bugs

Dealing with Frustration

LeetCode can be frustrating, especially when you're stuck on a problem for hours. Here's how I dealt with it:

Embrace the Struggle

Being stuck is part of the learning process. The problems that frustrate you the most often teach you the most. I learned to view difficult problems as puzzles rather than tests of my intelligence.

Take Breaks

When I was truly stuck, I'd take a break and come back later. Often, the solution would become clearer after some time away from the problem.

Learn from Solutions

There's no shame in looking at solutions after genuinely trying. The key is to understand why the solution works and how you might recognize similar patterns in the future.

Results and Lessons Learned

Quantitative Results

After 18 months of consistent practice:

  • Problems solved: ~300 (150 Easy, 120 Medium, 30 Hard)
  • Interview success rate: Made it to final rounds at 4/5 companies I interviewed with
  • Coding interview confidence: Went from terrified to actually enjoying the problem-solving process

Key Lessons

  • Consistency beats intensity: 30 minutes daily is better than 4-hour weekend marathons
  • Understanding > memorization: Focus on patterns and reasoning, not specific solutions
  • Communication is crucial: Practice explaining your thinking process out loud
  • Every problem teaches something: Even problems you can't solve provide learning opportunities

Advice for Beginners

Start Small

Don't jump to Medium problems if you're struggling with Easy ones. Master the fundamentals first. There's no shame in spending weeks on Easy problems—you're building a foundation.

Focus on Patterns

Instead of trying to solve every type of problem, focus on mastering one pattern at a time. Solve 10-15 problems using the same pattern before moving on.

Practice Coding by Hand

Some companies still use whiteboards. Practice writing code without IDE assistance—it forces you to think more carefully about syntax and logic.

Time Yourself

In real interviews, you typically have 20-30 minutes per problem. Practice under time pressure to build speed and decision-making skills.

Beyond LeetCode

While LeetCode is great for interview preparation, remember that real software engineering involves much more:

  • System design: Learn about scalability, databases, and distributed systems
  • Code quality: Practice writing clean, maintainable code
  • Debugging: Develop skills for finding and fixing bugs in larger codebases
  • Communication: Practice explaining technical concepts to different audiences

Final Thoughts

LeetCode isn't just about landing a job—it's about developing problem-solving skills that will serve you throughout your career. The logical thinking, pattern recognition, and persistence you develop will help you tackle any technical challenge.

Remember, everyone learns at their own pace. Don't get discouraged if progress feels slow. Focus on consistent practice, understanding fundamentals, and celebrating small victories along the way.

Most importantly, try to enjoy the process. There's something deeply satisfying about solving a challenging algorithmic problem—embrace that feeling and let it motivate you to keep improving.
