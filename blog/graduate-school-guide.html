A Complete Guide to Applying to Graduate Programs in CS and Engineering

December 10, 2024

Applying to graduate school can feel overwhelming, especially in competitive fields like computer science and engineering. Having recently gone through this process myself and helped several undergraduates with their applications, I wanted to share what I've learned about creating a strong graduate school application.

Start Early: Timeline and Planning

The graduate application process should begin at least 12-15 months before you want to start your program. Here's a rough timeline:

15-18 Months Before

  • Start researching programs and faculty
  • Begin building relationships with potential recommenders
  • Consider taking the GRE (if required)
  • Start or intensify research activities

12 Months Before

  • Finalize your list of target schools
  • Take the GRE and TOEFL (if applicable)
  • Begin drafting your statement of purpose
  • Request transcripts

6-9 Months Before

  • Reach out to potential advisors
  • Finalize your statement of purpose
  • Request letters of recommendation
  • Prepare your CV/resume

3 Months Before

  • Submit applications (deadlines are typically December-January)
  • Follow up with recommenders
  • Apply for fellowships and funding

Building a Strong Academic Profile

GPA: Quality Over Quantity

While a high GPA is important, admissions committees understand that different schools and programs have varying difficulty levels. What matters more is:

  • Upward trajectory: Improvement over time shows growth and dedication
  • Relevant coursework: Strong performance in courses related to your intended field
  • Research grades: Independent study and research course grades carry extra weight

Research Experience: The Golden Ticket

Research experience is arguably the most important component of a graduate application. It demonstrates:

  • Ability to work independently
  • Understanding of the research process
  • Specific technical skills
  • Genuine interest in academic work

How to get research experience:

  • Approach professors whose work interests you
  • Apply for summer research programs (REUs, etc.)
  • Join research labs as a volunteer initially
  • Pursue independent projects with faculty guidance

Publications: Quality Matters Most

Having publications as an undergraduate sets you apart, but remember:

  • One good publication > multiple weak ones
  • Conference papers and workshop papers count
  • Technical reports and preprints show productivity
  • Be honest about your contributions

Crafting Your Statement of Purpose

Your statement of purpose is your chance to tell your story and demonstrate fit. A strong SOP should:

Have a Clear Narrative

  • How did you become interested in your field?
  • What experiences shaped your research interests?
  • Why do you want a graduate degree?
  • What are your long-term goals?

Demonstrate Research Understanding

  • Discuss your research experiences in detail
  • Show understanding of the research process
  • Explain how your past work relates to future goals
  • Demonstrate knowledge of the field

Show Program Fit

  • Mention specific faculty you want to work with
  • Discuss how their research aligns with your interests
  • Reference specific papers or projects
  • Explain why this program is the best fit for your goals

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Being too vague about research interests
  • Not mentioning specific faculty or research areas
  • Writing the same statement for every school
  • Focusing too much on personal stories unrelated to research
  • Not explaining why you want a PhD (for PhD programs)

Letters of Recommendation

Strong letters of recommendation can make or break your application. Here's how to get the best letters:

Choose Recommenders Strategically

  • Research mentors: Professors who supervised your research
  • Course instructors: Professors from relevant courses where you excelled
  • Industry mentors: For professional master's programs

Help Your Recommenders Help You

  • Provide your CV, statement of purpose, and research papers
  • Remind them of specific projects you worked on together
  • Give them plenty of notice (at least 2 months)
  • Provide deadlines and submission instructions clearly

Reaching Out to Potential Advisors

For PhD programs especially, reaching out to faculty can be crucial:

Do Your Homework

  • Read their recent papers thoroughly
  • Understand their research methodology and interests
  • Check if they're accepting new students
  • Look at their current and past graduate students

Write a Compelling Email

  • Subject line: "Prospective PhD student interested in [specific research area]"
  • Briefly introduce yourself and your background
  • Mention specific papers of theirs that interest you
  • Explain how your research experience relates to their work
  • Ask if they're accepting students and if they'd be willing to discuss opportunities

Sample Email Template

Subject: Prospective PhD student interested in post-quantum cryptography

Dear Professor [Name],

I am a senior computer science student at UC Merced with strong research experience in cryptography. I am planning to apply for PhD programs for Fall 2025 and am very interested in your work on lattice-based cryptography.

I found your recent CRYPTO paper on "Efficient Implementation of CRYSTALS-Dilithium" particularly compelling, especially your approach to optimizing signature generation using AVX instructions. This aligns closely with my current research on SPHINCS+ optimization under Professor [Name], where we achieved a 56% performance improvement using similar vectorization techniques.

Would you be accepting new PhD students for Fall 2025? I would be very interested in discussing potential research opportunities in your lab. I have attached my CV and would be happy to provide additional information about my research experience.

Thank you for your time and consideration.

Program Selection Strategy

Create a Balanced List

  • Reach schools (2-3): Top programs where admission is uncertain
  • Target schools (4-6): Programs where you have a good chance
  • Safety schools (2-3): Programs where admission is likely

Consider Beyond Rankings

  • Faculty research fit
  • Funding availability
  • Location and cost of living
  • Program culture and environment
  • Industry connections and placement rates

Funding and Fellowships

Graduate school funding is crucial, especially for PhD programs:

Types of Funding

  • Research assistantships (RA): Work on faculty research projects
  • Teaching assistantships (TA): Help with undergraduate courses
  • Fellowships: External funding with fewer obligations
  • Industry partnerships: Some programs have industry-sponsored positions

External Fellowship Applications

  • NSF Graduate Research Fellowship Program (GRFP)
  • Department of Energy Computational Science Graduate Fellowship
  • Hertz Foundation Fellowship
  • Company-specific fellowships (Google, Microsoft, etc.)

The Interview Process

Some programs conduct interviews, especially for top candidates:

Preparation Tips

  • Review your research thoroughly
  • Prepare to discuss technical details
  • Practice explaining your work to different audiences
  • Prepare thoughtful questions about the program
  • Research the interviewing faculty's work

Common Interview Questions

  • "Tell me about your research experience"
  • "What challenges did you face and how did you overcome them?"
  • "Why are you interested in this particular program?"
  • "What are your long-term career goals?"
  • "How do you handle failure or setbacks in research?"

Making Your Final Decision

Once you receive offers, consider:

  • Advisor fit: The most important factor for PhD programs
  • Research opportunities: Availability of interesting projects
  • Funding package: Stipend amount and duration
  • Program culture: Collaborative vs. competitive environment
  • Location: Cost of living, weather, proximity to family
  • Career outcomes: Where do graduates end up?

Final Thoughts

The graduate school application process is challenging but manageable with proper planning and preparation. Remember that rejection doesn't reflect your worth as a researcher or person—admissions are highly competitive and sometimes arbitrary.

Focus on building genuine research experience, developing meaningful relationships with faculty, and clearly communicating your passion for research. With dedication and persistence, you'll find a program that's the right fit for your goals and aspirations.

Good luck with your applications! Feel free to reach out if you have questions about the process.
